## In 2028, the advisor shows up earlier

Laura reviews three business signals with Wanda, decides which one deserves a conversation, and calls Carlos before he applies for financing. Wanda organizes information and prepares alternatives; Laura interprets, asks, and recommends. Months later, Carlos calls her again before making another growth decision.

The proposal does not use AI to replace the commercial relationship. It reduces operational work that leaves advisors with less time to apply their judgment. I designed how the service could evolve and what conditions would support that change.

## The challenge: from handling requests to supporting decisions

In the service described by the challenge, advisors gather information across systems, prepare meetings, consult specialists, and follow up on opportunities. SMEs handle everyday needs through digital channels but seek human guidance when deciding how to grow. At that moment, the bank may have a valuable relationship with the client and still arrive too late or without enough context.

The design question was: **How might we anticipate a useful conversation without turning SME data into surveillance or adding another task to the advisor’s day?** The response was to redesign the service around moments when human judgment and relationships make a difference.

## Designing from a plausible future

I started with a 2028 scene: a client returns to their advisor before making a decision. Working backward from that future helped identify the capabilities, permissions, and choices that would need to come first. The horizon is a design tool, not a forecast. The proposal moves from a focused preparation brief to controlled trials and, later, to more integrated capabilities. Each stage depends on evidence and data governance.

### Method: from horizon to design decisions

The work moved through four steps. First, I framed the challenge as a tension between the promise of guidance and a service that may arrive too late. Then I explored change forces across social, technological, economic, and regulatory dimensions, distinguishing established trends from emerging signals. From these forces I developed possible futures and selected a preferred direction for its value to the relationship, not its certainty. Finally, backcasting translated that direction into capabilities, conditions, and progressive experiments.

The exploration considered social, technological, economic, environmental, and political-regulatory forces. It separated established trends from emerging signals, then used the two external uncertainties above to build scenarios. Backcasting from the preferred future informed capabilities, conditions, and progressive experiments. The methods informed one another; they are not evidence that the proposed service has been validated.

### Blueprint: connecting the promise to operations

The blueprint shows that arriving earlier depends on more than an interface. In the current service, the business signal may not be available to the advisor, preparation requires searching across systems, and credit follow-up can leave the client uncertain. In the proposed service, consent enables a signal, Wanda prepares context, and Laura decides whether there is a valid reason to start a conversation. Risk and operations retain responsibility for assessment and application status.

## An augmented system with human accountability

Wanda works in the advisor’s workspace. It gathers signals and their sources, prepares a view of the business, organizes alternatives, and flags a stalled application. Laura decides whether to contact the client, reviews the information, and owns the recommendation. The client does not talk to Wanda or receive decisions made by the system.

<ServiceSystemMap locale="en" caption="Service system map. The flows distinguish the client-advisor relationship from Wanda’s internal support and show the organizational capabilities required." />

The narrative prototype follows a Tuesday in this future through a signal card, preparation brief, proposal, and loan application status. These artifacts show how touchpoints might change. The story also sets clear boundaries: Wanda uses authorized data, shows its sources, and never contacts the client directly.

## How would we know whether this future deserves to scale?

The measurement framework links early signals to customer experience and business outcomes. A pilot would track whether advisors reach decision moments earlier, operational workload falls, and SMEs find the guidance more useful. Over time, I would measure customer recommendation, trust, advisor experience, and the bank’s role in financing decisions. These are proposed indicators, not project results.

The service would advance only if advisors choose to use it, signals are reliable and traceable, permissions remain valid, and trust does not decline. Results would be compared with a group not using Wanda. If an adverse effect appears, expansion stops even if operational indicators improve.

## What this exercise leaves behind

This exercise moved AI from the interface into the wider service. For the future to work, the organization also needs suitable incentives, data governance, operational integration, and a commitment to return saved time to conversations with SMEs.

The next step would be to review the scenario with advisors, SMEs, risk, and compliance. I would then test the preparation brief against real tasks and establish a baseline for evaluating change. That learning would help decide which capabilities to build and which to leave behind.
