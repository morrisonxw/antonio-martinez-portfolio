## Prevent discrepancies before billing

PASE transformed reconciliation between Mercado Libre and its logistics providers: from reviewing services weeks after execution to handling discrepancies during the active cycle. Reported follow-up showed that the same providers went from claiming approximately 30% of their services to 10%.

I worked for seven months as the lead designer, supported by a senior expert and collaborating with product, business, engineering and analysts. I conducted interviews, AS-IS and TO-BE modelling, journeys, workshops, prototyping and tests. I supported rollout in Argentina and Brazil, across First Mile and Last Mile, and the initial month and a half of follow-up.

## The problem: collect payment now, reconstruct later

Providers received a preliminary billing statement after accumulating between 20 days and a month of services. Even when they identified discrepancies, they moved ahead with payment documentation to sustain liquidity. In parallel, they reviewed records and claimed differences.

Monitoring showed the current day’s operation but did not retain the history needed later. Some analysts took screenshots at the end of the day. This was manual and incomplete: not every service had closed.

Templates requested codes useful to accounting but difficult for providers to interpret. A two-day submission window concentrated up to approximately 20,000 claims, mainly in Last Mile, with seven days to review them. I do not have an exact breakdown of that volume.

Adjustments generated supplementary statements for the next cycle, which could produce new discrepancies. Corrected cases could also be rejected because they appeared as already resolved. The process accumulated effort and made response reasons difficult to follow.

## Reframing and design decisions

Research showed that providers wanted the correct payment without disrupting operations. Product and experience reframed the challenge around the conditions causing claims, rather than simply speeding up their processing.

PASE offered history and daily review with recent information and comprehensible language. We discarded wider windows because they separated review from service context. We also discarded batch rejections: they could reproduce problems from the previous process.

The dependency on [PACTO, another initiative I contributed to](/en/projects/mercadolibre-pacto), was essential. Its structured agreements became valid after provider acceptance and supported checking recognised concepts. This connected commercial terms, execution and reconciliation.

## Automation with context and responsibility

AI interpreted information, resolved cases in the system under agreed conditions and provided explanations referencing agreements or operational records. A concept not contracted or explicitly excluded could, for example, be rejected automatically.

Cases that did not meet the conditions went to accounting. Accounting consulted operations when information was needed and retained responsibility for the response. Engineering implemented the technical conditions; my contribution was conceptual and experience-focused. “Absolute certainty” is not presented as a model guarantee.

<PaseArtifacts kind="process" locale="en" />

A version allowing operations to receive requests directly was planned for the next quarter. Its responses would reach the provider when accounting detected no issues, while accounting retained control. That evolution was outside the scope I supported.

## A tested decision: guide investigation, not the answer

Engineering and business proposed suggesting resolutions when the system could not resolve a case automatically. I identified the risk of conditioning an analyst’s judgement through an insufficiently supported recommendation. We tested the proposal and observed confusion.

I advocated an alternative: indicate what evidence to consult or whom to contact, without suggesting a resolution. This assistance reached production. Bias risk informed my argument; the observed finding was confusion, not experimentally measured bias.

<PaseArtifacts kind="employee" locale="en" />

Operations also raised questions about how to respond, share information with accounting and understand their responsibility. We revised copy and introduced training. Adoption required preparing teams’ work, not only designing the screen.

## The blueprint: information, handoffs and recovery

This blueprint was created after the project to explain the implemented service; it was not a method used originally. It aligns six journey stages with visible experience, internal work and supporting systems. Failure points show how service recovery worked.

<PaseArtifacts kind="blueprint" locale="en" />

After failures, reporting or resolution periods could be extended manually. If disagreement persisted after the response, providers continued through traditional claims, without reopening inside PASE. The transition relied on training and in-app help in both systems; the service incorporated PASE traceability.

This continuity was measured, but I do not have its results. Transferred fields and an automated handoff mechanism are not documented here.

## From decisions to interface

Volume made reviewing every service individually on screen impractical. I proposed an executive dashboard with filters and parameterised downloads to support aggregate review. Card exploration aimed to expose each route’s context and actions.

## What this case demonstrates

PASE connected commercial agreements, operational information, deadlines and responsibilities to address reconciliation before closing. My contribution combined research, modelling, facilitation, experience design and implementation support.

Results are reported by the participant and attributed to business, accounting and product; original datasets were not reviewed. Initial follow-up cannot establish long-term persistence. A timely response does not mean agreement, and a service without claims does not establish billing accuracy.

The learning was that automating a service also requires designing the work that stays with people: context to decide, clear boundaries and continuity when a response does not resolve disagreement.
