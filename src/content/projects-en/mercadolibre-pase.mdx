## Business context

Across first-, mid-, and last-mile transportation in Latin America, financial reconciliation happened reactively, weeks after trips were completed. That lag created high-impact financial losses for Mercado Libre: carriers had no transparency into logged routes or applied penalties — which flooded support channels — and part of the overpayments wasn't recoverable due to contractual restrictions that blocked retroactive deductions with large carriers.

## Research and insights

Through contextual interviews and support-data analysis in Mexico and Brazil, we found the core friction was an information asymmetry between Mercado Libre and carriers, compounded by traceability that degraded over time: reviewing a weeks-old trip meant manually rebuilding a trail with coordinators who no longer remembered the incident.

<ProductDivider eyebrow="Research" label="Reviewing a weeks-old trip meant reconstructing from memory something that should have been logged the same day." />

## Business rules and design decisions

That finding led to the contractual **Positive Silence** rule: routes not disputed within a 24-to-48-hour window from publication are automatically accepted. We also established a strict separation of domains: the system validates only physical service attributes (routes, kilometers, plate, driver, facility), leaving complex rate and penalty management to the commercial and financial systems.

Because the massive data volume collapsed the interface whenever we tried auditing trips one by one on screen, we opted for an executive panel with advanced filtering and parameterized downloads in structured formats (CSV/Excel) compatible with providers' accounting software.

## Result

The initiative returned financial predictability to the logistics chain: fewer unrecoverable discrepancies, and disputes resolved in hours instead of weeks. The underlying lesson was that in complex B2B tools, interface design alone isn't enough — it has to work hand in hand with restructuring contractual incentives and operational governance in the physical world.
