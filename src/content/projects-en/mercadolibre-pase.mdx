## Business context

Mercado Libre faced high-impact capital leaks from divergences that went undetected between the transport services third parties executed in the field and what the billing system recorded. Reconciliation happened reactively, weeks after trips were completed, creating two critical bottlenecks: carriers had no transparency into penalties or deviations, and part of the overpayments wasn't recoverable due to contractual barriers with large carriers that blocked retroactive deductions.

## Research and insights

Qualitative testing and contextual interviews in Mexico and Brazil revealed that reviewing a trip weeks old required manually rebuilding a trail with coordinators who no longer remembered the incident.

<ProductDivider eyebrow="Research" label="Reviewing a weeks-old trip meant reconstructing from memory something that should have been logged the same day." />

## Business rules and design decisions

That finding led to the contractual **Positive Silence** rule: a service not disputed within the stipulated window is automatically accepted. We rigorously separated the acceptance of physical attributes (routes, kilometers, plate, driver, facility) from rate and penalty management.

To keep the panel from collapsing under a massive volume of data, I ruled out item-by-item on-screen auditing in favor of an executive panel with advanced filters and parameterized CSV/Excel template downloads compatible with providers' accounting software.

## Result

The initiative returned financial predictability to the logistics chain: fewer unrecoverable divergences, and disputes resolved in hours instead of weeks.
