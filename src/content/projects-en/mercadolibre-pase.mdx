## The decision that changed the approach

PASE moved route reconciliation from a late financial review into the daily work of Mercado Libre and its transportation providers. My contribution was to translate that change into an experience for reviewing execution, identifying discrepancies and supporting decisions before billing.

The problem extended beyond the interface. Reviewing a trip weeks later meant recovering information that should have remained linked to execution. A faster screen would not resolve that time gap. The design decision was to bring review closer to the moment when teams could still explain what had happened.

## Work profiles, not invented biographies

I separated three roles to organise needs. Providers support a discrepancy with evidence; operations assess it; business and finance intervene when decisions exceed an operational rule. This distinction informs permissions, information and next actions.

The cards start from tasks mentioned in the case, not validated segmentation. Usage frequency, autonomy and prioritisation criteria need to be checked through [interviews and observation].

## The provider journey

The journey follows one perspective: a provider coordinator who needs to clarify a route discrepancy before billing. It first organises actions, then identifies touchpoints, and finally connects friction to a proposed experience.

Keeping one perspective avoids mixing user actions with internal service work. No emotional curve is drawn because no supporting data is available. Thoughts and emotions remain [information to validate].

<PaseArtifacts kind="journey" locale="en" />

The opportunity is to retain context and a comprehensible next action for each discrepancy. Requesting evidence is not the same as resolving a case; a proposed adjustment is not a recorded reconciliation. That distinction informs states and confirmations.

## A target process with decisions and loops

The following map is a proposal from this review, not a recovered historical artifact. It makes explicit the branches that the narrative did not detail: matching records, incomplete evidence, human review and requests for another review.

The sequence starts with recorded execution. Matching records can only proceed under an [authorised rule]. When a discrepancy exists, the provider adds context. Missing evidence triggers a request without pretending that the case is resolved. Operations or finance make decisions requiring human authority.

## From structure to interface

When volume made individual trip review impractical, I proposed an executive dashboard with filters and parameterised downloads. The decision addressed aggregate review rather than adding more information to every row.

The card exploration supports examination of how context and actions are presented per route. The design question is whether users understand what they are reviewing and what they can do next. Answering it requires testing; the image does not replace that evidence.

## How to evaluate the proposal

The measurement plan separates speed, discrepancies, timeliness and rework. It first requires defined events and comparable cohorts, then enables observation of changes. No outcome charts are drawn because original data is unavailable.

Proposed tests cover a route without discrepancies, incomplete evidence, a financial exception and a reopened case. Alongside task success, they should capture critical errors and comprehension of states. [Participant count], [success criterion] and [advancement threshold] must be agreed before testing.

## Learning and the next review

The design contribution was to bring reconciliation closer to execution and connect the interface with rules and responsibilities. This review strengthens the case explanation without attributing new outcomes or turning a proposal into project history.
