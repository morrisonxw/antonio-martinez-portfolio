## Context

Providers saw the pre-invoice as a black box, which overwhelmed support with questions the product should have answered on its own.

## My role

I led the full UX process: I took the findings from the research team's discovery and turned them into product flows and screens. I aligned with product on how the billing area worked, did research with analysts and business, and designed and prototyped hypotheses that we iterated together until we found a definition that hit the target and was viable for the teams, a process that had to coexist with manual and regulatory processes that couldn't simply be removed.

## How I worked

Research showed that most users were only looking for three things: the amount, the payment date, and the ability to download a file compatible with their own accounting software. Everything else competed for attention with those three answers.

<ProductDivider eyebrow="Insight" label="Most users just wanted to confirm how much they were being credited, when they'd be paid, and a clear breakdown to identify discrepancies on their own." />

I prioritized simplifying over adding: I removed heavy tables, rewrote the process timeline with language that didn't assume internal context, and restructured the downloadable file to be readable without relying on support. That alone drastically cut support requests about payment status.

We also identified, together with the billing team, an opportunity outside the original scope: some payments were managed separately, in parallel documents. I built the flows and prototyped the direct integration into the same pre-invoice, distinguishing the base payment from the adjustments, and we tested them until everything was consolidated into a single document, and the parallel modality is now used only for edge cases.

<ProductDivider eyebrow="Design decision" label="Every parallel settlement was one more question the provider had to ask support. Consolidating the document removed the question." />

Less reliance on support, fewer disputes, and a simpler accounting close for providers: the peace of mind of understanding their own payment without outside help.
