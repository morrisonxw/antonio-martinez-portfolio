## Business context

Logistics providers saw the pre-invoice as an incomprehensible black box, which flooded support channels with questions about dates and amounts the product should have answered on its own. On top of that, extraordinary payments — extra tolls, weight surcharges, waiting time — were settled separately, in parallel documents disconnected from the regular billing cycle: more uncertainty for the provider, accounting patches, and double claims work for Mercado Libre.

## Research

Research showed that 95% of carriers never checked the line-by-line detail in the browser: they came to the pre-invoice looking for exactly three things — the total amount due, the estimated payment date, and the ability to download the full file in a format compatible with their accounting software. Everything else on screen was noise competing for attention with those three answers.

Separately, managing additional payments outside the regular cycle generated a steady stream of complementary invoices that disrupted fiscal closes and raised flags for internal auditors.

<ProductDivider eyebrow="Insight" label="95% of users just wanted to confirm how much, when, and have the proof to back it up." />

## Design decisions

We removed the heavy tables that dominated the screen and added a visual tracker with clear stages of the settlement process — pre-invoice issued, review period, approval, payment order sent — replacing confusing internal jargon with language a carrier could follow with no prior context on Mercado Libre. We also restructured the downloadable settlement file to be readable without relying on support, and did UX writing on every message in the flow.

## Consolidating complementary payments: Single Invoice

While clarifying the view, we found that extraordinary payments were managed separately, in parallel settlements the provider had to reconcile on their own. We designed the integration of these already-approved items directly into the same pre-invoice, clearly distinguishing the base regular payment from approved adjustments, consolidating the Single Invoice model: one document, one payment date, no loose settlements.

## Result

Clarifying the pre-invoice and consolidating complementary payments into a single document reduced reliance on support, lowered disputes over charges that used to arrive disconnected from the rest of billing, and simplified the accounting close for logistics providers, at a point in the process that used to generate distrust. For providers' accounting teams, no longer having to chase down explanations about their own payment brought back something simple: the peace of mind of understanding, without outside help, how much they'd be paid and when.
