## Business context

Logistics providers' users saw the pre-invoice as a hard-to-understand black box, which led them to flood support channels with questions about payment dates and amounts that weren't showing: questions the product should have answered on its own, but didn't. On top of that, extraordinary payments (extra tolls, weight surcharges, waiting time) were settled separately, in parallel pre-invoices disconnected from the regular billing cycle, adding more uncertainty for the provider, accounting friction, and double claims work for Mercado Libre and our providers.

## My role

**UX/UI Design / Senior Product Designer.** I led the user experience, supporting the research expert through the pre-invoice's initial research, then translating findings into definitions, generating and iterating proposals from low to high fidelity, and supporting strategic alignment with the Billing team to adapt operational processes and incorporate complementary payments into the regular invoice.

## Research

Research from the team showed that 95% of carriers never checked the line-by-line detail in the browser: they came to the pre-invoice looking for exactly three things: the total amount due, the estimated payment date, and the ability to download the full file in a format compatible with their own accounting software. Everything else on screen was low-value noise competing for attention with those three answers.

Separately, managing additional payments outside the regular cycle generated a steady stream of complementary invoices that disrupted fiscal closes and raised flags for internal auditors.

<ProductDivider eyebrow="Insight" label="95% of users just wanted to confirm how much we were crediting them in the pre-invoice, an approximate payment date, and a clear breakdown of services so they could identify discrepancies internally." />

## Design decisions

We removed the heavy tables that dominated the screen and improved, at the content level, the timeline with clear wording about the settlement process stages (pre-invoice issued, review period, approval, payment order sent), replacing confusing internal jargon with language a carrier could follow with no prior context on Mercado Libre. We also restructured the downloadable settlement file to be readable without relying on support, and did UX writing on every message in the flow.

## Consolidating complementary payments: Single Invoice

While clarifying the view, we identified an opportunity in the extraordinary payments that were managed separately, in parallel settlements the provider had to reconcile through independent flows. We designed the integration of these already-approved items directly into the same pre-invoice, clearly distinguishing the base regular payment from approved adjustments, consolidating the Single Invoice model: one document, one payment date, no loose settlements.

## Result

Clarifying the pre-invoice and consolidating complementary payments into a single document reduced reliance on support, lowered disputes over charges that used to arrive disconnected from the rest of billing, and simplified the accounting close for logistics providers, at a point in the process that used to generate distrust. For providers' accounting teams, no longer having to chase down explanations about their own payment brought back something simple: the peace of mind of understanding, without outside help, how much they'd be paid and when.
