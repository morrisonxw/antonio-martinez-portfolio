## Context

Thousands of billing claims came in through non-standardized forms, with no traceability for carriers and no real control for analysts.

## My role

This was the first big project I took on at the company, in two stages, covering the full UX process: discovery, flow and interface design, prototyping, and testing. In the first stage I ran discovery with the management and provider teams to map the existing process and design the new one, alongside a product role and a technology manager. When we identified the limitations of that first version, I took the initiative again to design the rebuild on proprietary technology together with the team.

## How I worked

We started by mapping the existing process together with analysts and providers to define the new one, prioritizing adapting a tool the company already had to move fast. Over time, that inherited base showed technical limits and wore the team down managing thousands of cases one by one — the sign that the team needed to rethink the solution from scratch, not just iterate it.

I built the flows and prototyped a standardized queue with automatic validations, designed to process in batches instead of case by case, and we tested it with analysts until it freed them up for the cases that actually needed human judgment.

I also redesigned rejection notifications, replacing internal technical language with explanations a carrier could understand without escalating to an analyst.

## Learning

The first version prioritized individual traceability from day one, but that turned out to be unsustainable both technically and experience-wise. Together with the team, we learned to let go of features that seemed valuable internally but users didn't perceive that way, and to pivot toward the model that actually sustained the real volume.
