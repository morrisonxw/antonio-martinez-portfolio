## Business context

Over 20,000 billing claims came in per period, through unstandardized Excel forms sent over email and Jira. That flow overwhelmed internal analysts, lost information along the way, and left carriers with no visibility into their claim's status.

## Product evolution

The project went through two distinct stages. In the first — systematization and integration — I ran discovery with analysts and providers to map the as-is process and define the to-be, and led a comparative analysis of existing internal tools to speed up implementation, adapting the design to a ticketing system the company already had.

After two years running on that setup, we found the inherited tool had technical limitations, performance issues, and was wearing analysts down as they tried to manage thousands of cases individually. That led to the second stage: rebuilding the system on proprietary technology, to gain ownership of the code, remove unused flows, and add detailed tracking features.

## Claims queue

We built a standardized, parameterized claims queue — the one shown on this project's cover — with automatic contract validations, integrated with the Solver rules engine, to process in batches and resolve direct refunds without case-by-case manual intervention.

## UX writing in notifications

We pivoted from an individual-ticket view toward a template-based bulk claims model, with batch management via parameterized queries for analysts. In parallel, we redesigned rejection notifications, replacing system codes with clear explanations tied to the applicable contract clauses, so a rejected claim could be understood without escalating to an analyst.

## Learning: the MVP-to-batch tradeoff

We initially shipped an individual-management MVP, designed to guarantee systemic traceability from day one. It became unsustainable at the technology level, and the experience for the Business Control team wasn't a good one. We made the tradeoff of cutting features we internally saw as valuable but users didn't perceive that way, and pivoted to the batch upload and management model that ended up sustaining the real volume.
