## Context

Design and development teams were building interfaces from a static brand guide in PDF format: every new requirement meant designing and coding components from scratch, causing visual inconsistencies and contractual delays.

## My role

I led the account's UX squad and built and documented the first master library of living components, working closely with frontend development and external vendors.

## How I worked

I started by cataloguing, together with the squad, the recurring components and patterns across active applications to prioritize what to build first, instead of trying to document the whole system at once. I structured the library under WCAG accessibility standards from the design stage, not as a later review.

I represented Intergrupo in technical alignment sessions with EPM's other technology vendors to drive adoption of a single system, and mentored junior designers on the squad. Sustaining a shared library also means the rest of the team trusts using it.

Unlike other projects, here I didn't have direct access to external users: research was run by outside vendors contracted for that purpose, and I built the library from the insights they shared, plus what I could observe firsthand in a handful of usability sessions.

## Result

The design and development team stopped rebuilding the same thing over and over, and gained a shared, reliable foundation to build on.
