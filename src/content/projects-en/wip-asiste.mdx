## Context

WIP, formerly Asiste, is a tool for home-services companies to manage their field personnel. The platform suffered high churn from a confusing information architecture, and the corporate website didn't communicate the value proposition or convert visits into business opportunities. I came in as Lead Product Designer on two parallel fronts: redesigning the product's core to reduce that churn, and restructuring the acquisition website so it actually converted.

## Process

I interviewed five operations analysts to identify the most relevant friction points, and cross-referenced those findings with data from users who had already cancelled their subscription or were actively complaining. Interviews with logistics coordinators showed the real priority was simple: knowing where personnel were, the status of the service, and photographic proof of what was done. Nothing more.

From there, I simplified the web panel and led a team of 2 designers in building a lightweight mobile app for field operators, built for mid- and low-end phones: buttons adapted for on-route touch use, a service list, navigation to the job site, fast evidence uploads, and offline functionality (a non-negotiable requirement for staff working outside coverage), plus tools for analysts to monitor the operation and each field collaborator's status in real time.

To keep up that design pace while directing the team, I also built the company's first component library: it unified the visual experience across screens and sped up development of new features.

For the acquisition website, we restructured the sales narrative around directly resolving the customer's pain point, and mapped out the entire demo-booking flow together with marketing, from the form to automated reminders, so leads wouldn't fall through the cracks from lack of follow-up.

## Result

Lower churn among existing users, and sustained growth in weekly sign-ups through the redesigned website. Field operators gained the autonomy to get through their day without depending on calls to a coordinator, and coordinators got real visibility into the team without chasing manual updates. In usability testing, 90% of users completed tasks without needing assistance: the clearest sign that simplifying down to the essentials had been the right call.
