## Context

WIP — formerly Asiste — is a tool for home-services companies to manage their field personnel. The platform had a high churn rate due to poor operational usability, and the corporate website wasn't converting visits into business opportunities. I came in as product designer on two parallel fronts: redesigning the product's core to reduce that churn, and building an acquisition website that actually converted.

## Process

I interviewed five operations analysts to identify the most relevant friction points, and cross-referenced those findings with data from users who had already churned or were actively complaining. Interviews with logistics coordinators showed the real priority was simple: knowing where personnel were, the status of the service, and photographic proof of what was done. Nothing more.

From there, I simplified the web panel and led a team of 2 designers in building a lightweight mobile app for field operators, with buttons adapted for on-route use, a service list, navigation to the job site, evidence uploads, and offline functionality — a non-negotiable requirement for staff working outside coverage — plus tools for analysts to monitor the operation and each field collaborator's status in real time.

## Result

Lower churn among existing users, and sustained growth in weekly sign-ups through the redesigned website. In usability testing, 90% of users completed tasks without needing assistance — the clearest sign that simplifying down to the essentials had been the right call.
